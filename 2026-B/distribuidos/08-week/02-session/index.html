Planning — story mapping, estimation and MVP 2 commitment

Halfway through Corte 2, planning gets sharper: you map the product's stories to a coherent journey, estimate them honestly with relative sizing, untangle cross-service dependencies, and commit only what fits — so MVP 2 (integrated communication + persistence) lands on time.

1

Build a story map of the product journey.

2

Estimate with relative sizing (planning poker).

3

Sequence work around cross-service dependencies.

4

Commit a realistic MVP 2 scope.

1. Story mapping: see the whole journey

Note

A story map lays the user's journey left-to-right (the backbone of activities) and details/priorities top-to-bottom. It shows the thinnest end-to-end slice (the release line) and what waits below. It keeps the team building a usable path, not disconnected features.

2. Estimate relatively, not in hours

Note

Estimate size/complexity/uncertainty with story points on a Fibonacci-ish scale (1,2,3,5,8,13). Use planning poker: everyone reveals at once, discuss the outliers (that discussion is the real value). An 8+ is a smell — split it. Track velocity (points/sprint) to forecast, never as a target to game.

3. Untangle cross-service dependencies

Caution

In a multi-service product, stories depend on each other across teams/services (Orders needs Inventory's GET /stock). Map dependencies and sequence so nobody is blocked. The classic deadlock: two services each waiting for the other's endpoint. Break it with the contract first (agree the API now, mock it, build both sides against the mock).

4. Commit realistically

Note

Commit velocity-worth of Musts that serve the sprint goal, with a little buffer for the unknown. Leftover Should/Could stay in the backlog. Overcommitting is the #1 reason sprints fail — a smaller finished increment beats a larger half-done one.

5. A path you will face in real life

Caution

Scenario — optimism + a hidden dependency. The team commits 40 points (they've never done more than 25) and assumes Payments' webhook "will be ready". Day 4: the webhook isn't ready, half the stories are blocked, the sprint collapses. Fix: commit near your real velocity; for the dependency, agree the contract now and build against a mock/stub so Orders proceeds without waiting; make the dependency itself a tracked story with an owner and a due date. Plan for reality, not hope.

6. Common mistakes

  • Estimating in hours and treating them as commitments.
  • Ignoring cross-service dependencies until they block the sprint.
  • Overcommitting far beyond velocity.
  • Feature list with no end-to-end journey (story map missing).
