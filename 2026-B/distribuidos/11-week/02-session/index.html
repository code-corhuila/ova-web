Planning — SLIs, SLOs, error budgets and alerting

Observability data is only useful if you decide what "healthy" means and what to do when it isn't. This planning session defines your SLIs and SLOs, the error budget that balances reliability with shipping speed, an alerting policy that avoids fatigue, and the runbooks that turn a 2 a.m. page into a checklist.

1

Define SLIs and SLOs for the product.

2

Use an error budget to balance reliability vs. speed.

3

Design symptom-based alerts (no fatigue).

4

Write a runbook for a likely incident.

1. SLI, SLO, error budget

Note

An SLI (Service Level Indicator) is a measured number, e.g. "% of requests < 300ms" or "% non-5xx". An SLO (Objective) is the target, e.g. "99.5% of requests succeed over 30 days". The error budget is what's left (100% − SLO = 0.5%): the allowed unreliability. Spend it on shipping features; if you blow it, you slow down and stabilize.

2. Choose SLOs users actually feel

Tip

Pick a few SLOs tied to user experience: availability (success rate), latency (p95/p99), and for async flows, freshness (how stale a read model may be). Avoid vanity metrics. Write them down with the measurement window (e.g. 30 days).

3. Alert on symptoms, with a policy

Caution

Alert on what users feel (error rate up, p99 latency up, SLO burn-rate) — not on every CPU tick. Each alert needs: a clear trigger, a severity, an owner/on-call, and a runbook link. Too many low-value alerts cause alert fatigue — people ignore the pager, and the real one gets missed.

4. Runbooks turn panic into a checklist

Note

A runbook is a short doc per likely incident: symptoms, how to confirm, first mitigations (e.g. flip the feature flag, scale up, fail over), how to escalate, and how to verify recovery. It lets anyone on-call act calmly at 2 a.m.

5. A path you will face in real life

Caution

Scenario — the pager that cried wolf. The team alerts on every CPU>70% and every single 500. Fifty pages a night; everyone mutes the channel; then a real outage (checkout error rate 40%) is missed for an hour. Fix: delete cause-based alerts, alert on SLO burn rate and user-facing error/latency, attach a runbook to each, and route by severity. Fewer, meaningful alerts beat a noisy wall — reliability is a planning decision, not an afterthought.

6. Common mistakes

  • SLOs that don't reflect user experience (vanity metrics).
  • Alerting on causes (CPU) → fatigue, missed real incidents.
  • Alerts with no owner and no runbook.
  • No error budget → endless firefighting or endless caution.
